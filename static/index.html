Your location
Signed decimal degrees: north/east positive, south/west negative.
35° means the angle above the horizontal plane at this location. 0° is the horizon; 90° is directly overhead.

          
When to alert
Every timing applies only to passes that reach your minimum elevation. Default: 5 minutes before horizon rise.
Uses Windows’ default playback output and unmutes it. Enabled by default.
Real alerts stay visible until acknowledged in the app. Acknowledging silences that pass. Sounds also stop when the satellite drops below the minimum. Test alerts last up to 60 seconds and use the dashboard banner.

          
Satellites & frequencies
Add, edit or remove satellites here. Frequencies are in MHz, at the satellite before Doppler correction. Linear defaults use the centre of the passband. Leave uplink empty for receive-only operation.
Add satellite
Satellite edits save immediately and stop live equipment tracking. Base frequencies are editable references; geometry does not establish whether a transponder is operating.

Orbital data
On startup, missing data, a download at least 1 day old, or watched elements older than 1 day trigger an immediate refresh. Default: a fresh download every 24 hours. Failed downloads retry after 5 minutes. Alerts are suppressed for elements older than the age limit.
Source: AMSAT nasabare.txt ↗

          
Rotator · Yaesu GS-232B
Pointing uses azimuth and elevation together. A new direction is sent only when either angle changes by at least 1°. Reported antenna position is read every 5 seconds. Match the controller’s baud rate and calibrated travel limit.

          
Radio · Icom IC-9700
COM6 and 57600 are used when radio settings are unset. Match the baud rate on your radio/interface; higher USB CI-V rates require USB baud unlinked from REMOTE. Existing choices are retained. Set USB SEND and AFC off and clear RIT/ΔTX offsets. Avoid turning the tuning dial during live tracking; the PC adjusts both bands independently. Set any required CTCSS tone on the radio.
The app adjusts RX on MAIN and TX on SUB, selects modes and checks frequency readback. It does not key PTT. Leaving uplink empty uses receive-only VFO operation.

          
Live tracking
The highest current elevation wins when passes overlap. The same satellite drives the rotator and radio. Start or stop adjustments with the button at the top of the live view. Alert pauses affect notifications; equipment tracking has its own switch.
Tracking starts off after each app restart. Changing equipment, frequency, station or watch-list settings stops tracking; use Start live tracking when ready.

          
Automatic pause
Default: midnight to 7am, every day. The pause starts at your chosen local time and ends after this many clock hours, including across midnight.
Resume alerts overrides the current automatic pause until the next daily pause starts. Manual Pause stays in effect until you press Resume. Changing this schedule clears the current Resume override.

How Satellite Alert works
35° above your local horizon
Elevation is calculated from your saved latitude, longitude and height. The app uses Skyfield and the SGP4 orbit model to turn AMSAT’s two-line orbital elements into a direction and distance from your ground station. The dashed ring marks your minimum elevation. Satellites inside that ring are high enough to meet it.
Keep the app running
The Python monitor checks positions every 2 seconds and fetches orbital data every 24 hours. Leave the program running and the computer awake. On Windows, the bundled WAV chime and optional popup work even with the browser closed. Press Test alert to check your speakers and enable browser sound as an additional sound source. The default Windows volume setting raises the playback output to 100% and unmutes it before the chime; disable it in Settings to use your current volume. Enable browser sound is available on Windows too. On macOS or Linux, keep the dashboard open and enable browser sound after each reload. Check tab mute, volume and your speaker output if the test is silent. Closing the terminal or pressing Stop app ends monitoring.
Choose the moment
The default alerts 5 minutes before horizon rise for passes whose predicted peak reaches your minimum elevation. You can also choose 10 or 20 minutes before rise, at horizon rise, or when the satellite reaches the minimum elevation. The early alert works while the satellite is still below the horizon. A running app also catches a qualifying pass already underway when you start or resume. Acknowledging an alert prevents another alert for that pass. Real alerts stay in the app banner until acknowledged, including after a Windows message is closed or the pass ends. Use Acknowledge & silence above any app page, or Acknowledge next to the alert in Alert history.
Daily automatic pause
Settings → Automatic pause sets a daily start time and a number of hours. The default is midnight to 7am, using this computer’s local clock. Positions and orbital data keep updating. Resume alerts overrides the current automatic pause until the next daily pause begins. Manual Pause stays in effect until you press Resume. Manual control survives restarting the app. Changing the schedule clears its Resume override.
The watch list shows the earliest next unalerted qualifying pass first; satellites without a predicted alert go last. Pausing does not change predicted pass times.
Live Earth and sky
Click the Earth or Expand live view for a larger map. Earth mode plots each visible satellite at its calculated geographic ground position, with your station at the centre. Select Your sky for local azimuth and elevation. All watched satellites above the horizon appear; the highest current elevation is larger and gold. Positions update every 2 seconds.
Rotator and radio control
Settings lists the detected serial ports and lets you configure a GS-232B rotator and IC-9700 CI-V radio. Use Start live tracking on the main page to begin; Stop live tracking stops the rotator and closes the connections. Test radio connection alternates the MAIN frequency between 145.000 and 145.555 MHz every 2 seconds until pressed again, with a yellow progress label. Starting this test stops live tracking. Tracking covers the whole pass above the horizon and chooses the highest current satellite for both devices. Equipment control always starts off after restarting the program.
The rotator receives combined azimuth/elevation changes only after either angle changes by at least 1°. Radio RX and TX get separate Doppler corrections, with a configurable frequency deadband. The live panel shows satellite angles, reported rotator angles and verified radio frequencies. The satellite editor adds, edits and removes entries with base RX/TX frequencies, modes and notes. Leave TX empty for receive-only tracking. See the README for radio setup, tones and protocol details.
Pause alerts affects notifications; the separate live-tracking button controls equipment. Hardware or satellite edits stop live control. A rotator error, including a missing C2 reply, stops the rotator while radio Doppler corrections continue. The equipment panel shows the separate rotator error. Stop and restart live tracking to retry it. A radio error stops live control. Protocols were tested using simulated devices; check controller calibration and tuning with your equipment.
Test the rotator connection
Test rotator connection on the main page sends one C2 query through your saved rotator port and baud rate. The result shows the test time, raw reply and reported angles, or a warning if no port is configured. Live tracking and radio updates continue. This query does not move the antenna or restart a failed rotator.
Calculated frequencies and radio readback
Calculated RX and TX frequencies continue changing even with live control off. Last verified RX and TX show the radio readback, with its time, satellite and pass update count. Serial failures appear at the top of the main page. Correct the issue and press Start live tracking again. At the start of each satellite, the app checks the radio bands and exchanges MAIN and SUB when the downlink band is on SUB before tuning it.
Optional Earth background
Switch the Earth image on or off directly in Your Sky. It is shown by default. The image is a decorative background; the satellite path and elevation ring still show your local sky. Your choice is saved and changing it does not silence an active alert.
Fresh elements matter
These are predictions. Atmospheric drag, manoeuvres and old elements can shift pass times. At startup it checks the download age and watched satellites’ element epochs, refreshing if data is missing or older than 1 day. The app keeps the last good download during a connection failure and shows its age. It suppresses alerts for orbital elements older than the configured limit. Geometry alone does not mean a satellite is sunlit, visible to the naked eye, or operating its radio.
Copy to another computer
Extract the supplied ZIP, install Python, run Install.cmd once, then Start.cmd. The README includes full Windows, macOS and Linux instructions. Settings are in settings.json; downloaded elements and the alert history are in data/.
